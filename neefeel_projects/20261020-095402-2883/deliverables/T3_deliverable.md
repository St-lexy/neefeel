## 3. Drafting notes — read before approving

**3.1 The ₦0 cap is the weakest clause in the document.** A cap set at nil means a user has effectively no remedy, and Nigerian courts — plus the Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Act 2018 — may treat that as an unfair term or as an unreasonable limitation, particularly for a consumer-facing tool. If the cap falls, exposure is not capped at zero; it is uncapped. A nominal floor (e.g. ₦10,000–₦25,000) is often more defensible than zero. I have drafted the clause as instructed (cap at fees paid = ₦0) and flagged it rather than quietly changing it. **This is a decision for you and counsel, not for me.**

**3.2 This is a disclaimer, not a Terms of Service.** It disclaims advice and liability. It does not cover acceptable use, IP ownership of the tool, account/auth terms (none exist yet), termination, or the freemium terms Finance is hypothesising (unapproved). If InvoiceLite goes beyond PoC, this notice sits *inside* a ToS, not instead of one.

**3.3 Scope creep is a liability event.** The Engineering build deliberately excludes PDF export, auth, multi-user, and server persistence. Every one of those additions changes this document — PDF export adds a "the generated document may render differently" issue; auth adds credentials and account data; server persistence triggers clause 9 immediately. Treat any of those as a disclaimer-revision trigger, not a feature ticket.

**3.4 No analytics, no third-party scripts, no fonts-from-CDN.** If anything is added that phones home, clause 9's clean "we do not receive it" statement becomes false and the document is misrepresenting the data flow. That statement is the strongest protection in the draft and the easiest to accidentally break.

**3.5 Prominence matters.** A disclaimer buried in a footer is weaker than one presented at first use. For a PoC, a one-time acknowledgement on first launch ("InvoiceLite is a calculator, not tax advice — you are responsible for your invoices") materially improves enforceability of the notice as a whole. That is a product change, not a legal one — flagging it for Engineering.

**3.6 Counsel review is required before any publication.** This draft is an internal starting point, grounded in the actual InvoiceLite build. It is not legal advice to you, and it is not a substitute for a Nigerian-qualified lawyer reading it.
